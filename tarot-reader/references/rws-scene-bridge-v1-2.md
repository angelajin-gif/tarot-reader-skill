# Bounded RWS scene bridge

Use this reference only after a canonical card has resolved and its frozen visual packet has loaded successfully. It improves user-facing language; it does not add fields to the packet or change any frozen observation.

## Three layers

Keep these layers distinct even when the final prose is fluid:

1. **Frozen visual fact** — exactly what the `rws.factual.*` packet records.
2. **RWS scene identification** — the specific participant, animal, object, or coherent visible action established by the canonical RWS card identity together with those facts.
3. **Interpretation** — the motif, psychological operation, relationship hypothesis, or forecast inferred from the scene and the rest of the spread.

The second layer may be more natural and specific than the first. It may name a lion instead of “large feline,” or describe a clear combined action instead of reciting hand coordinates. It must not silently become the third layer.

## Strength example

- Frozen packet floor: a figure bends close to a large feline; both hands are near the animal's head and mouth.
- RWS scene identification: a woman leans close to a lion and gently handles or closes its jaws, taming or containing it without a violent struggle.
- Interpretation, only when warranted by the question and spread: the image may support a contrast between coercion and gentle self-command, or between raw impulse and conscious containment.

In Chinese user-facing prose, prefer a sentence such as: “力量牌不是一个人碰巧站在大型猫科动物旁边，而是一位女子贴近狮子，以温和的动作合拢、约束它的嘴；我把这幅‘不靠搏斗而驯服力量’的场景读作……”

Do not claim that the image visibly proves “inner strength,” that the woman is the querent, or that she will tame another person. Those are interpretive claims and require the question, spread relations, counterevidence, and bounded language.

## Stop conditions

Keep the generic packet wording when the species, identity, action, or interaction is not securely resolved. Never fill uncertainty from another deck, a remembered variant, a user-uploaded image, or an invented story. If the scene identification materially affects the judgment, label it as RWS scene identification and keep the exact packet fact adjacent.

A static pose is not proof of motion stage, intent, or duration. For example, a horse shown in a standing composition can be described as posed with its legs on the ground; do not turn that into “it has stopped,” “it will remain still,” or a claim about its exact gait when the packet leaves gait unresolved.
